# Overnight vs intraday returns, intraday patterns, calendar seasonality

Research date: **2026-11-11** (system clock; the brief is dated 2026-11-10). Every URL below was accessed in this one session on that date.
Scope: what the literature establishes about (1) the overnight/intraday split of returns, (2) intraday patterns that matter on 1h and 4h charts, (3) calendar effects, and what of it a Pine script on a Premium plan can compute at bar close without repainting. No data was tested here: this is a reading report, and nothing in it is a trading recommendation.

## 1. Findings that change design decisions

| # | Finding | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The index-level "night effect" cannot be harvested by trading the open and the close. The best-identified version (S&P 500 futures, 2:00–3:00 a.m. ET, 3.7% a year over 1998–2020) had a Sharpe ratio of 1.1 before costs and −0.5 after bid-ask spreads, and its own authors report that the window "has averaged close to zero since 2021". Two ETFs built on it ran from June 2022 and closed fourteen months later. | [PAPER] P7; [DOC] P8 |
| 2 | Stock-level overnight and intraday components are strongly persistent **within** the same leg and reverse **across** legs (next-month overnight alpha +3.47%, intraday alpha −3.02% for the past-overnight-return decile spread, 1993–2013). The two legs nearly cancel close to close, and the authors themselves say costs make trading it "much less attractive". For a swing trader it is a stock characteristic (who is buying this stock, and when), not a strategy. | [PAPER] P1 |
| 3 | Every "overnight" number depends on how the open is measured. Overnight returns are "lowest using VWAP" and lower when the first five minutes are included; with a midquote open the P1 overnight alpha falls from 3.47% to 1.88% a month. What TradingView's daily `open` is for a US stock (auction print, first trade, which venue) was not verified. | [PAPER] P1, P2; unverified for TradingView |
| 4 | Market intraday momentum (first half-hour predicts last half-hour) "disappears in the out-of-sample period". It is not a basis for a 1h-chart signal. | [PAPER] I3 |
| 5 | Most calendar effects in the brief are dead or sign-unstable in liquid US stocks: turn of the month "disappears entirely after 2001"; the pre-holiday premium is insignificant for the value-weighted index after 1990 and for the S&P 500 over 1983–2019; the weekend effect is "not reliably different" from other days since 1978; the FOMC-cycle pattern of 1994–2016 was followed by "a statistically significant opposite cycle" in 2017–2021. | [PAPER] C2, C5, C6, C10 |
| 6 | The pre-FOMC drift is contested and at best much smaller: 49 bp per meeting in Sept 1994–Mar 2011; "essentially disappeared after 2015" in one study; "about 20 bps" overnight before the announcement over Apr 2011–Dec 2023 in another, reverting after the announcement. | [PAPER] C8, C9, C10 |
| 7 | The calendar-type results with the best recent support are flow-based, not date-based: a one-standard-deviation rebalancing signal from a 60/40 portfolio predicts −16 to −17 bp for equities the next day (1997–2023), reverting within two weeks; and same-calendar-month return seasonality in the cross-section (13% a year, long-short, gross). Both are computable in Pine. | [PAPER] C13, C15; [COMPILE] for the building blocks |
| 8 | Volume on intraday bars must be compared with the same time-of-day slot: the first half hour carries 14.25% of regular-session dollar volume (1993–2013) and the closing auction alone 7.5% of daily volume in 2018 (3.1% in 2010). TradingView ships this normalisation as `relativeVolume()` in its `ta` library, version 14. | [PAPER] P1, I6; [DOC] T4, T5; [COMPILE] |
| 9 | Realised variance from 5-minute intrabars is the benchmark estimator ("difficult to significantly beat 5-minute RV") and is computable with `request.security_lower_tf()`; Premium's 100,000-intrabar ceiling limits it to roughly five years of 5-minute bars, so an OHLC estimator (Yang–Zhang) is needed for longer history. | [PAPER] I9, I10; [DOC] T3; [COMPILE]; history length is arithmetic from T7 |
| 10 | Execution, not signals, is where this literature pays a swing trader: buying attention stocks at the open carries a hidden cost that "frequently exceed[s] the effective half spread"; closing-auction prices deviate 2.66 bp on average for large stocks (8.12 bp all stocks) and revert overnight; intraday losers bounce in the last half hour (+4.8 bp a day for the extreme decile, 1993–2024). All are a few basis points per trade. | [PAPER] P5, I6, I5 |

### 2.2 Can a trader who executes at the open and the close exploit it?

| Strategy | Gross | Trades needed | Net at 5 bp per side | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hold the index only overnight (buy the close, sell the open, daily) | About 7.2% a year as the overnight-minus-intraday gap in S&P 500 stocks (P13) | 252 round trips a year | Costs 252 × 10 bp ≈ 25% a year (inference). In futures, Sharpe 1.1 before and −0.5 after bid-ask spreads (P7) | Dead |
| Same, real-money evidence | NightShares NSPY and NIWM launched June 2022; "Both funds were closed fourteen months later" (P8). Performance since inception −4.72% and −7.61%, stopped trading 31 July 2023 (P15) | – | – | [DOC] P8; [SECONDARY] P15 |
| Long-short the overnight leg of past overnight winners and losers | 3.47% a month ≈ 16.5 bp per day (inference: ÷21); 1.88% a month ≈ 9 bp per day with a midquote open | Both legs turned over at every close and every open | 2 round trips a day × 10 bp = 20 bp a day (inference), more than the gross | Dead; P1: "transaction costs will make the actual profitability ... much less attractive" |
| Hold past overnight winners close to close | Legs offset: +3.47% − 3.24% ≈ +0.2% a month excess return (inference from P1's two components) | Monthly | Indistinguishable from zero | Not a strategy |
| After a daily-close alert, fill at the next close instead of the next open (skip one day session) | Stock-specific: the average S&P 500 stock earned 2.75 bp more overnight than intraday per day (P13); top-beta decile +20 bp night against −8 bp day (P3, equal-weighted, includes small stocks) | None extra: only the timing of one entry | A one-off timing gain of a few bp to a few tens of bp per entry, if it still exists | Worth one test (H4) |

## 6. Where the literature or the practitioners are wrong or contested

| # | Issue | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "All of the market's return is earned overnight" is a statement about one definition of the open. It shrinks with a VWAP open or when the first five minutes are included, cannot be earned after costs, and its most precise form went flat in 2021–2025 | [PAPER] P2, P7; [DOC] P8 |

## 8. Hypotheses to test

Conventions: signals are computed at the close of a daily bar unless stated; entry at the next regular open; costs 5 bp per side (10 bp doubled as stress); universe = the project's liquid US stock set; forward returns measured open to close-of-day-N so that they are tradable. "Prior" is my probability that the effect has the stated sign and remains economically meaningful out of sample after costs. Twelve hypotheses mean twelve trials for the registry before any variant is counted.
